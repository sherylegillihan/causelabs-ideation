Case Study

Arts & Culture Web Platform For Wichita Falls Symphony Orchestra Wichita Falls, TX

Designing a digital experience worthy of the music.

The website wasn't the experience.

Performing Arts Nonprofit Ticketing & Events

For the Wichita Falls Symphony Orchestra, the experience begins long before the conductor raises a baton.

It begins when someone wonders what's playing this season.

When a parent looks for an opportunity to introduce their child to live music.

When a longtime patron wants tickets.

When a local business considers sponsoring a concert.

When someone who has never attended a symphony asks a very human question: What should I expect?

The Wichita Falls Symphony Orchestra needed its website to serve all of those people.

But years of adding information, programs, events, ticketing details, education resources, and giving opportunities had created a familiar problem: the organization had grown, but the digital experience hadn't grown with it.

The challenge wasn't simply to redesign a website.

It was to make the WFSO easier to experience.

Finding the real problem

A symphony website has an unusual number of audiences.

There are subscribers and first-time concertgoers. Donors and sponsors. Parents and educators. Students and musicians. Volunteers and community partners.

Each arrives with a different question.

So rather than beginning with pages, CauseLabs began with people and intent.

We reorganized the experience around the things visitors actually come to WFSO.org to do:

Discover a performance. Buy tickets. Plan a visit. Learn about education programs. Support the orchestra. Understand the organization behind the music.

That distinction mattered.

A website structured around an organization asks visitors to understand how the organization works. A website structured around its audience does the opposite. It understands them.

Then the season changed the rules

There was another wrinkle.

For the 2025–2026 season, WFSO performances weren't happening in one familiar concert hall. Concerts were being presented at different venues across Wichita Falls.

Suddenly, information that might once have been secondary became essential.

Where is this concert? Where do I park? What should I expect when I arrive? Which performance is right for me? How do I buy the right ticket?

The website couldn't merely advertise concerts. It needed to reduce uncertainty between discovering a performance and sitting down to enjoy it.

That insight shaped the new experience.

Designing pathways instead of pages

CauseLabs redesigned WFSO.org around clear audience journeys.

Season information and events became easier to explore. Ticket purchasing became more prominent. Plan Your Visit content helped bridge the gap between interest and attendance.

Education programs were brought forward as a significant part of WFSO's community role rather than treated as secondary information.

That role is substantial.

WFSO's Young People's Concert introduces more than 1,500 students each year to live symphonic music. Its MUSE Program provides complimentary admission to classical performances for local middle- and high-school orchestra and band students and their chaperones.

The redesigned site gives programs like these room to tell that larger story.

Because WFSO isn't simply producing concerts.

It's building an audience for music across generations.

Support needed a story, too

The same thinking changed how we approached giving.

Ticket sales cover only a portion of what it costs to operate the orchestra. WFSO relies on its Annual Fund and other community support to fund performances, musicians, guest artists, educational programs, and outreach.

Yet "Donate" alone doesn't explain any of that.

The redesigned Support Us experience shows people the different ways they can participate: annual giving, sponsorships, special and planned gifts, advertising, volunteering, and ticket donations.

That's an important shift.

Fundraising isn't just a transaction.

It's an invitation to participate in what happens next.

The payoff: a website that behaves more like WFSO

The finished website does something deceptively simple.

It helps people find their way in.

A first-time visitor can discover a concert and understand what to expect. A parent can find opportunities for a young musician. A business can understand sponsorship opportunities. A patron can support the orchestra beyond buying a ticket.

And WFSO has a digital platform capable of telling a much larger story about its impact across Wichita Falls and the Texoma region.

The technology matters. The information architecture matters. Accessibility, responsive behavior, content structure, ticketing integrations, and all the invisible machinery underneath matter.

But none of those things are the point.

The point is that technology should remove friction between people and something worth experiencing.

For WFSO, that something is music.

For CauseLabs, that's what thoughtful technology is supposed to do.

What could your website make easier?

If your organization's website has accumulated years of programs, audiences, priorities, and content, the problem may not be that you need a prettier website.

You may need a clearer path between the people you serve and the reason your organization exists.
